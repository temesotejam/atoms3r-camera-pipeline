# Phase-matched foot observation and bounded energy candidate

The 1.0.58 run 67227721 used an 8 degree setting but remained at a 5 degree gradual-start reference for most of the run. Its positive-side selection was almost always 400 mA / 80 ms, while the left foot showed a substantially larger camera-relative excursion. A prior cycle-based foot adaptation could not start for the active extrapolated model branches. Foot frame log time and peak time use different recording epochs; use the measurement-relative frame timestamp for peak alignment.

For target-8 wheel-work mode the observer matches both calibrated relative foot angles to each positive and negative body peak using frame timestamps within 100 ms. A complete negative-positive-negative cycle computes left as positive minus the mean of the surrounding negative values, and right as the mean negative value minus positive. Values are signed; these are body-camera-relative phase indicators, not ground step distances. Consecutive valid cycles are required. The positive/left association is supported by six logged runs; the physical sensor sign to right/left body tilt was not independently confirmed.

When the measured left excursion exceeds right and both sides have valid measured recent output, a restricted probe may lower the existing positive branch-5, 400 mA candidate by 5 ms. It does not add motor power, change the nominal energy target, widen the existing current or duration envelope, or activate when speed or battery telemetry is invalid. Real executed actions and both body peaks are tracked. A further 5 ms step, up to a total reduction of 15 ms, requires observed improvement over two subsequent complete cycles without unacceptable loss of movement or opposite-side peak; otherwise the candidate unwinds or stops. Missing observations, loss of negative-side output, changed target, unstable or reversed response, mode change, current/direction mismatch, or emergency STOP interrupt the experiment. A mode change may skip one positive command before returning to the ordinary selector, so not every fallback is a 5 ms transition. Any actual current/sign mismatch requires a whole-run emergency stop. This is an experimental online intervention, not a proven causal model.

The ordinary energy candidate is computed without the previous low-peak width adjustment or previous two-peak MPC. The old low-peak learner is diagnostic only. This version is not a multi-peak MPC and does not command negative-side power outside its existing guard. Neither cycle excursion nor yaw is directly measured against the ground, and straight walking is not guaranteed. The camera frame timestamp has not been independently verified as an exposure timestamp.

Independent timestamped replay of 67227721 and 175997260 (with bounded notification delays) reaches an 80 to 75 ms phase-cap candidate and a valid 75 ms branch-5 model evaluation. Replays stop at the first changed candidate: the unchanged recorded trajectory cannot predict what happens after an intervention. The tests do not establish timing under live load, actual motor current response, opposite-foot dynamics, heading, or physical safety on this robot.

Subsequent review found that this replay accepted extrapolated foot angles outside the calibration range. The earlier 80-to-75 ms result is therefore not evidence that the same intervention will occur when only in-range observations are accepted. In addition, a comparison of adjacent two-cycle averages in recorded runs 139742122 and 175997260 found absolute changes with medians of about 0.46 and 0.53 degrees. These are observed variations under changing inputs and walking states, not estimates of sensor noise or intervention effects. They do show that the current 0.10 degree, two-cycle improvement criterion cannot by itself establish that an intervention caused improvement. The physical response to a pulse change and ground-relative heading still require controlled real-world measurements.
