# Guarded low-wheel-speed walking energy branch

The 1.0.56 walk (139742122) remained in phase 0 for foot identification, as expected because positive predictions were cross-condition/saturated and negative commands were repeatedly unsupported. Between 5 and 20 seconds, one side reached 4.91 degrees on average, the other 4.35 degrees; negative inputs were skipped 6 times out of 19. At 10.449, 12.788, 15.167 and 17.548 seconds the pre-input wheel speeds were 269.70, 264.50, 257.96 and 262.06 rpm, just below the former 274.90 rpm lower domain edge.

A post-hoc check of four runs (139742122, 31971838, 44337328, 38726609) found 26 unpowered, same-side-next-peak matches within 240–274.90 rpm and the existing rate, previous peak and voltage bounds. The measured potential against the old coast model gives RMSE 0.0277 mJ, MAE 0.0228 mJ and max absolute 0.0553 mJ. This is a post-hoc selection, not independent randomized validation; samples within runs are correlated. The fixture records the source hashes and each match. It does not prove the response to powered input or a turning mechanism.

The separate experimental branch 11 accepts the existing zero-CG, target-8 geometry/rate/voltage domain with wheel speed 240–274.90 rpm. It starts at zero output, even if the older branch has learned from other speeds. Only two individually matched, low-speed coast arrivals, separated by no more than four seconds and consistent with the model, can qualify a 10 ms / 300 mA cap. Intervening normal branch arrivals may retain qualification only while fresh and within RPM/target/prediction limits; unsupported or unmatched negative peaks clear it. A separate 1.5-second limit between all matched negative peaks catches missing cycles, while intermediate peaks do not extend the four-second low-coast limit. Capped trials can increase 5 ms after two matched arrivals, with a maximum of 25 ms; deteriorating predicted-energy residual, overshoot, stale/wrong wheel speed, invalid prediction or missing pairing cancels/locks the extension. This is a cautious online guard, not causal validation. The residual window is larger than the predicted incremental input, so apparent agreement alone cannot prove the pulse increased the energy.

The wider branch is explicitly cross-condition and does not enable the independent foot feedback. The exact input/peak event, branch, RPM, cap, and coast sample count are logged. Existing 10/12 degree modes, commands outside this narrow branch, motor stop conditions and pulse-current limits remain unchanged. Successful compilation and offline tests do not validate timing, real wheel response, gait or heading on the robot.
